Lighting

Immersive Dance Lighting Rig

I packed two hundred fixtures into the ceiling, but running them at full brightness emptied the dance floor.

About the project

There was an ambient power that emanated from the rig. It was all black and incredibly dense. I had never packed so much equipment into such a small space before. When the room was dark, the hardware vanished. It felt like a beast was sleeping up there. The owner wanted a video ceiling running the length of the room, curving down behind the DJ. The designer wanted the support pillars encased in LED strips. I added my own lighting layer, dropping in nearly two hundred moving fixtures.

Getting it wired and working was half the battle. Getting programming that looked pro was another beast entirely. I spent weeks integrating the video ceiling, the pixel pillars, and the movers so a couple of operators could actually run it. We had a monstrous rig, and it was tempting to use all of it at once.

But when we finally opened the room, we found out what happens when you do. Running everything at full brightness emptied the dance floor. It was not just about the raw intensity, though that was part of it. It was the ratio of fixtures firing simultaneously. People felt exposed. If you want people to dance, you have to let them feel like no one is watching. We dialed the overall output way back. We learned to let individual elements emerge from the blackness, keeping the crowd in the dark where they belonged.
